Gold and Shares Across Four Monetary Periods

Gold futures rose during four monetary periods examined in The Hidden Regressive Tax, a paper by The Prehn Institute. Their percentage increase was smaller than the S&P 500’s in the first three periods and larger in the fourth.

Felix Nikolas Prehn, economist, tech founder and former investment banker, examines the changes alongside figures for the Federal Reserve’s balance sheet, the M2 money stock, consumer prices, wages and house prices. The comparisons cover different dates, so each percentage describes the change within its own period.

The Four Periods

The first period runs from September 2008 to December 2014 and covers three Federal Reserve asset purchase programmes. The second, from December 2008 to December 2015, covers a period of near-zero policy rates. Although both begin in 2008, their different start and end dates mean they record different movements.

The third period covers the pandemic asset purchase programme from February 2020 to March 2022. The fourth runs from January 2021 to August 2026, overlapping with the pandemic period.

Each percentage records the cumulative change over its period. The periods vary in length and overlap, so they cannot be combined into a single longer-term return.

Gold and the S&P 500

During the 2008 to 2014 asset purchase period, gold futures rose 35 per cent and the S&P 500 rose 77 per cent. Consumer prices increased 8 per cent over the same months.

In the near-zero-rate period, gold futures rose 20 per cent, while the S&P 500 rose 126 per cent. Consumer prices increased 12 per cent. Gold recorded its smallest increase of the four periods, while the S&P 500 recorded its largest increase across the first three.

During the pandemic programme, gold futures rose 25 per cent. The S&P 500 increased 53 per cent and consumer prices rose 11 per cent. In each of the first three periods, the increase in gold futures exceeded the rise in consumer prices but remained below the increase in the S&P 500.

The fourth period showed a different relationship. From January 2021 to August 2026, gold futures rose 143 per cent and the S&P 500 rose 107 per cent. Consumer prices increased 27 per cent.

The Monetary Measures

The Federal Reserve’s total assets and M2 measure different things. The first measures the size of the central bank’s balance sheet, while M2 is a broader measure of the money stock. Their movements did not always match.

During the first three periods, the Federal Reserve’s total assets rose by 393, 113 and 115 per cent respectively. M2 increased by 49, 51 and 41 per cent. In the fourth period, the Federal Reserve’s total assets fell 8 per cent, while M2 rose 20 per cent.

Fiscal transfers and supply disruptions also affected prices during the pandemic, alongside changes in monetary conditions.

Reading the Price Figures

The gold figures refer to COMEX front-month futures. The gold and S&P 500 figures use monthly closing prices, while the Federal Reserve’s total assets use the nearest weekly observation to the cited month.

Gold’s performance relative to the S&P 500 changed across the four periods, while the Federal Reserve’s balance sheet and M2 moved in different directions in the final period. The Institute also examines consumer durables within household assets. The full paper, The Hidden Regressive Tax, includes the wider comparisons and data.
